1. Overview ​

TitleFighter Force
GenrePuzzle · colour-matching auto-shooter
PlatformWeb (mobile portrait first)
EnginePhaser 4, TypeScript, Vite
Session1–4 minutes per level

1.1 Pitch Live ​

A packed hangar of colour-coded fighter planes sits under an enemy armada. Tap a fighter to send it up to the flight zone, where it lines up under an enemy of its own colour and fires straight up. Enemies at the front shield the ones behind them, and every enemy shoots back at the flight zone, which has limited HP. Only fighters with a clear path out of the hangar can take off, and only 5 can fly at once. The puzzle is the order: launch the right colours for the front line in front of you, fast, and don't fill the sky with fighters that have nothing to shoot.

1.2 Design pillars ​

  1. Every tap is a decision. The player never aims; all skill is in choosing which fighter to launch next, and launching it before enemy fire wears the flight zone down.
  2. Readable at a glance. Colour, a number (ammo on fighters, hit points on enemies) and a dimmed/bright state tell the whole story.
  3. Always fair. Every level is built so each colour's ammo exactly equals that colour's enemy hit points, and a solver proves every shipped level can be won (see Levels & balance).

1.3 Target player ​

Casual mobile puzzle players who enjoy sort, jam and unblock games (one-thumb play, short sessions), plus a light shooter fantasy for visual payoff.

1.5 Art direction Live ​

Glossy arcade space shooter, in the style of mobile "galaxy attack" games: dark space, metallic ships, neon light.

ElementLook
BackgroundDeep navy-to-purple nebula with two star layers scrolling down at different speeds (18 and 55 px/s) for parallax
FightersFighters that can launch idle with a gentle bob and burning engines. One colour tone only: fuselage, wings, canopy, guns and engine flame are all tints and shades of the fighter's colour (no grey, gold or blue). The ammo number only appears once the fighter is in the flight zone
Regular enemiesBat-winged drones (small, medium) and spiked heavies (large): armour in the enemy's colour, dark metal body, glowing core, orange engine flames
BossOne complete spaceship in one of three designs (raptor warship, mantis hive ship, dreadnought battle saucer), nose down. Each part is a section of the airframe painted in its colour (wings, central hull with reactor, nose, engines with flames, outer gun pods) on a dark metal hull. The guns on each section show its weapon (single, triple spread, long burst barrel, aimed twin barrels). Destroyed sections go dark and their engine flames go out
BulletsFighters fire neon plasma bolts in their colour, straight up; enemies would fire spinning glowing diamonds in theirs (enemy fire is off for now)
EffectsAdditive light: muzzle flashes, hit sparks, explosions with a white core, coloured bloom, shockwave ring and sparks
Hangar and UIDark glass panels with chamfered corners and glowing cyan edges; landing-slot cells; walls are metal crates with hazard stripes; the flight zone is a cyan energy-shield band; stage progress is a row of dots with a BOSS badge in the HUD
Home screenJust the title FIGHTER FORCE (with a cyan glow) over the hero fighter, and a PLAY button
ElevatorMetal lift shaft with a glowing chevron on the side it faces, its remaining count, and a dim preview of the next fighter
Mystery fighterNear-black silhouette with a violet rim and a big "?"; reveals with a coloured flash
Frozen fighterTranslucent faceted ice block with cracks over the fighter and a large counter; shards fall at each count, and the block bursts at 0
FontRusso One (Google Fonts), falling back to Arial Black

Colour is still the gameplay read: every ship, plate and bullet is painted in its gameplay colour, so the art never hides which fighter can hit what. All art is painted in code at start-up (src/game/art.ts) with the Canvas 2D API, so there are no image files to manage yet.
